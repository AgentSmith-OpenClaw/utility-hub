# How to Calculate Mortgage Affordability and Survive the Stress Test (UK & Canada)

Buying a house isn't just about saving the deposit. It's about proving to a bank that you won't default when interest rates spike. Enter the mortgage stress test. 

## What is a Mortgage Stress Test?

Banks don't care if you can afford the mortgage today. They care if you can afford it when things go bad. 

In Canada, OSFI requires banks to test your affordability at 5.25% or your contract rate plus 2%, whichever is higher. If you are offered a 4.5% rate, the bank calculates your monthly payment as if your rate were 6.5%. 

In the UK, the FCA scrapped the mandatory stress test rule recently, but major lenders still apply their own buffers, usually adding 1% to 3% to their standard variable rate. 

## Calculating Your Debt Service Ratios

Lenders look at two numbers:
1. **Gross Debt Service (GDS):** The percentage of your pre-tax income that goes to housing costs (mortgage, heat, taxes). This should stay under 39% in Canada.
2. **Total Debt Service (TDS):** Housing costs plus all other debt (car loans, credit cards). This needs to be under 44%.

If you make $100,000 a year pre-tax ($8,333 a month), your total debt payments cannot exceed $3,666 a month. And remember, that mortgage payment is calculated at the stress test rate, not your actual rate.

## How to Beat the Test

You have three levers:
- **Increase your income:** Side hustles or getting a co-signer.
- **Decrease your debt:** Pay off that car loan before applying. A $400 monthly car payment destroys your borrowing power.
- **Increase your down payment:** Less principal means a smaller stress-tested payment.
