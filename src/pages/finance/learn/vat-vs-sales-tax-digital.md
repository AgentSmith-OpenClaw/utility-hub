# VAT vs Sales Tax for Digital Products: What Founders Need to Know

Selling software, ebooks, or courses online? You are now an unpaid tax collector for dozens of governments. It sucks, but ignoring it will cost you massive penalties. Here is how VAT in the EU compares to Sales Tax in the US.

## The US Sales Tax Nightmare

The US has no federal sales tax. Instead, we have 50 states, thousands of counties, and local municipalities all setting their own rules.

If you hit "economic nexus" in a state (usually $100,000 in sales or 200 transactions), you must register and collect tax there. But it gets worse. Some states tax SaaS, others don't. Some tax ebooks, others tax only downloaded software. You absolutely need a tool like Stripe Tax or TaxJar to handle this. Doing it manually is impossible.

## The EU VAT System: Simpler but Stricter

The European Union uses Value Added Tax (VAT). For digital goods, the rule is clear: you charge VAT based on the customer's location, not yours. 

If you are a US company selling a $50 ebook to a customer in Germany, you must collect 19% German VAT. Yes, even if you have no physical presence in Europe. 

The EU made this easier with the VAT OSS (One Stop Shop) scheme. You register in one EU country (Ireland is popular for English speakers) and report all your EU sales through that single portal.

## The B2B Exemption

Here is the good news. In the EU, if you sell to another business with a valid VAT number, you don't charge VAT. It falls under the "reverse charge" mechanism. The buyer handles it. Always collect VAT numbers during checkout for your European customers.
