# High-Yield Savings vs. Money Market Funds: Where to Park Cash in 2026


Cash is no longer trash, but leaving it in a traditional checking account earning 0.01% is financial self-sabotage. As the Fed adjusts rates in 2026, the spread between High-Yield Savings Accounts (HYSAs) and Money Market Funds (MMFs) dictates where your emergency fund should live.

## Understanding the Difference

An HYSA is a bank product. It is FDIC insured up to $250,000. The bank sets the rate, and they can drop it at a moment's notice.

A Money Market Fund is an investment product (mutual fund) offered by brokerages like Vanguard, Fidelity, or Schwab. It invests in short-term government debt. It is not FDIC insured, but it is backed by SIPC and the underlying assets are generally US Treasuries—the safest assets on earth.

## The Tax Advantage of MMFs

This is where MMFs win for high-income earners. If you buy a Treasury-only MMF, the interest generated is generally exempt from state and local income taxes. 

If you live in California or New York, an HYSA paying 4.5% might actually yield less after taxes than a Treasury MMF paying 4.3%. 

### How to Allocate Your Cash Today
1. **The 1-Month Buffer:** Keep one month of living expenses in an HYSA linked to your primary checking account for instant liquidity.
2. **The Core Emergency Fund:** Keep the remaining 3-6 months in a Treasury-backed MMF at a major brokerage. 
3. **The Yield Chase:** Stop jumping banks every time one offers a 0.1% higher HYSA rate. The time spent opening accounts and moving funds is rarely worth the marginal return. Use MMFs, which automatically track the highest available short-term yields.

## Glossary of Terms
*   **APR (Annual Percentage Rate):** The true cost of borrowing, including fees.
*   **Cost Basis:** The original value of an asset for tax purposes.
*   **Yield:** The income return on an investment.
*   **FX Spread:** The hidden fee banks charge when converting currencies.
