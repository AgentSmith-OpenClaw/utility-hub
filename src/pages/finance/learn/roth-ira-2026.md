# Maximizing Your Roth IRA Contributions in 2026

The Roth IRA remains the most powerful tax-advantaged account for US investors. The rules changed slightly in 2026, and you need to adjust your strategy to avoid penalties.

## The 2026 Contribution Limits

The IRS increased the base contribution limit to $7,500. If you are 50 or older, the catch-up contribution is $1,000, bringing your total to $8,500. Income phase-outs also shifted upward. Single filers start phasing out at $150,000 modified adjusted gross income (MAGI), while married couples filing jointly phase out at $240,000. 

## The Backdoor Roth Strategy

If you earn above the MAGI limit, you cannot contribute directly. You must use the backdoor Roth IRA method. 
1. Open a Traditional IRA.
2. Make a non-deductible contribution up to the limit.
3. Immediately convert the funds to your Roth IRA.

This legally bypasses the income restrictions. The pro-rata rule applies if you have existing pre-tax Traditional IRA balances. Clear those balances by rolling them into your 401(k) before December 31st to avoid a tax hit.

## Asset Location

Keep your highest-growth assets in the Roth IRA. Since all withdrawals are tax-free in retirement, you want your aggressive stock index funds (like VTI or IVV) here. Keep bonds in your pre-tax accounts.
