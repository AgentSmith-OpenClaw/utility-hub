# Canadian First Home Savings Account (FHSA) 2026 Guide

The Canadian housing market is absurd. Trying to save a down payment in Toronto or Vancouver while paying rent feels like a joke. But the government threw a massive tax break at first-time buyers with the FHSA (First Home Savings Account). 

If you are not using it, you are literally giving up thousands in free tax money.

## What is the FHSA?

It combines the best parts of an RRSP and a TFSA. It was introduced a few years ago to help you save for your first home faster.

You can put up to $8,000 per year into an FHSA, with a lifetime maximum of $40,000. 

Here is why it’s completely overpowered:

1.  **Tax-Deductible Contributions**: Like an RRSP, every dollar you put in reduces your taxable income. If you earn $80,000 and put $8,000 in an FHSA, the CRA taxes you as if you only made $72,000. That means a massive tax refund in the spring.
2.  **Tax-Free Withdrawals**: Like a TFSA, when you pull the money out to buy a house, you don't pay a single cent of tax on it or any of the investment gains.

## How to Maximize the $8,000 Limit

You get $8,000 of room every year starting the year you open the account.

If you don't have $8,000 to drop in right now, open the account anyway. Just put $1 in it. Your contribution room starts accumulating. You can carry forward up to $8,000 of unused room to the next year. 

If you opened it last year and put nothing in, you can contribute $16,000 this year and get a gigantic tax deduction.

## Where to Open an FHSA

Don't open an FHSA at a big bank and leave it in a 1% savings account. Inflation is currently running higher than that. You need the money to grow.

Open it at a discount brokerage like Wealthsimple or Questrade. They charge zero commission fees to buy ETFs. 

### What to Invest In

If you are buying a house in two years, do not buy stocks. The market could crash 20% right before your closing date. Put the money in guaranteed investments.

*   **CASH.TO**: This is an ETF that holds your money in high-interest savings accounts across big Canadian banks. It pays out around 4-5% in interest every month. It is incredibly safe.
*   **GICs (Guaranteed Investment Certificates)**: You lock your money in for 1-5 years and get a guaranteed 4.5% to 5.5% return.

If your timeline is five to ten years out, you can take more risk with broad market index funds like XEQT or VGRO. But as you get closer to buying, move it back to CASH.TO or GICs.

## The Fine Print

1.  You must be a Canadian resident, 18 or older.
2.  You cannot have owned a home in the part of the calendar year before the account is opened or at any time in the preceding four calendar years. 
3.  If you don't end up buying a house after 15 years, you can transfer the entire balance (including all the gains) directly into your RRSP without using up any of your existing RRSP room. You literally cannot lose with this account. 

If you don't own a home and might want one someday, open an FHSA today.
