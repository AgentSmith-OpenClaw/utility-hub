# Navigating the First Home Savings Account (FHSA) in Canada

The FHSA combines the best features of an RRSP and a TFSA. Contributions are tax-deductible, and withdrawals to buy a qualifying first home are tax-free.

## Contribution Rules

You can contribute up to $8,000 per year, with a lifetime limit of $40,000. Unused contribution room carries forward, but only up to a maximum of $8,000 per year. If you open an account and contribute nothing in year one, you can contribute $16,000 in year two. 

## Qualifying for the FHSA

You must be a Canadian resident, 18 or older, and a first-time homebuyer. You are a first-time homebuyer if you did not live in a home that you or your spouse owned in the current year or any of the four preceding calendar years.

## Investments

Hold the same investments you would in a TFSA: stocks, bonds, mutual funds, and GICs. Given the short time horizon for buying a home, prioritize capital preservation. High-interest savings ETFs (like CASH.TO) offer solid yields without equity market risk.
