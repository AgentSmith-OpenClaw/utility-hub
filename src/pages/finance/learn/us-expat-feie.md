# US Expat Taxes: The Foreign Earned Income Exclusion

The United States taxes based on citizenship, not residency. If you live abroad, you still have to file a US tax return. The Foreign Earned Income Exclusion (FEIE) prevents double taxation.

## The FEIE Limit for 2026

For the 2026 tax year, you can exclude up to $130,000 of foreign-earned income from US taxation. This applies only to earned income (salary, wages, self-employment). Passive income like dividends, capital gains, and rental income cannot be excluded.

## The Physical Presence Test

To qualify, you must pass either the Physical Presence Test (PPT) or the Bona Fide Residence Test (BFR). 

The PPT requires you to be physically present in a foreign country for 330 full days during any 12-month period. Travel days do not count. The BFR requires you to establish a permanent tax home in a foreign country for an uninterrupted period that includes an entire tax year.

## Foreign Tax Credit (FTC)

If you live in a high-tax country (like Germany or the UK), the Foreign Tax Credit is usually better than the FEIE. You take a dollar-for-dollar credit against your US tax bill for taxes paid to the foreign government. This strategy allows you to contribute to a Roth IRA, which the FEIE prevents.
