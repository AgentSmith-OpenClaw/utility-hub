# The UK Energy Market is Broken: How to Actually Save on Your Bills

I’ve spent the last week looking at the UK energy market data for early 2026, and it’s worse than I thought. The price cap system we have in place is a band-aid on a gaping wound. We’re told we’re protected, but the reality is that millions are still paying far more than they should for basic heating and electricity.

The fixed tariffs being offered right now are predatory. Energy companies are betting against you, and they usually win. Here is exactly what is happening and what you should be doing instead.

## The Fixed Tariff Trap

Let's look at the numbers. Suppliers are offering 12-month fixed tariffs that sit just below the current price cap. It feels like a deal, right? But what they aren't advertising is that wholesale gas prices are projected to fall by the end of the year. If you lock in now, you are almost guaranteed to overpay through the winter of 2026-2027.

They know this. That’s why the exit fees have crept up. It used to cost £30 to leave a bad contract; now you're looking at £75 per fuel.

You can't afford to guess on your energy bills anymore. The margin for error is gone. Here is the framework you need to apply today.

1. **Calculate Your Exact Usage:** Don't rely on the "average user" figures. Your house is unique. 2. **Ignore the 'Green' Premium:** Many "100% renewable" tariffs are simply standard electricity matched with REGO certificates. They buy cheap certificates and charge you a premium. Don't pay extra for greenwashing. Find the absolute cheapest tariff, regardless of the marketing.
3. **Invest in Efficiency First:** The cheapest energy is the energy you don't use. Stop chasing a £20 saving on a new tariff when you're losing £200 a year through your windows. Use the [Toolisk Home Efficiency Planner](/tools/home-insulation-roi) to see which upgrades (loft insulation, draft excluders) actually pay for themselves within 18 months.

## Demand Better

The regulator isn't coming to save you. The energy companies certainly aren't going to offer you a better deal out of the kindness of their hearts. You have to be ruthless with your calculations. If the numbers on a fixed tariff don't save you at least £100 a year based on pessimistic wholesale projections, stay on the variable rate. 

The market wants you confused and scared of the next price hike. Beat them by knowing your numbers better than they do.
