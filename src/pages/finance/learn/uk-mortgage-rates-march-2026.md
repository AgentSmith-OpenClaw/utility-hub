# Fixed vs. Tracker Mortgages in March 2026: The New Reality of UK Interest Rates

For UK homeowners and buyers, March 2026 has brought a bit of a curveball. While many expected the Bank of England (BoE) to be well into a rate-cutting cycle by now, a recent "Energy Shock" has stalled those plans. The BoE's decision to hold rates has left many wondering whether to lock in a fixed rate now or take a gamble on a tracker.

## Why the "Dovish Hold" Matters

A "dovish hold" usually means the central bank is keeping rates steady but signaling that cuts are coming soon. However, inflation data from February was higher than forecasted, mainly due to rising gas and electricity prices. This has shifted the market's expectations. Instead of multiple cuts this year, we might only see one or two—if we're lucky.

## Fixed vs. Tracker: The Pros and Cons

Choosing between these two options is currently a balance of risk and stability.

### Fixed-Rate Mortgages
Fixed rates are still the most popular choice for a reason: certainty. With a fixed rate, your monthly payment won't change, regardless of what the BoE does. 
*   **Best for:** People who need a predictable budget and don't want to worry about their mortgage costs rising if inflation stays high.
*   **The Downside:** If the BoE does manage to cut rates significantly later this year, you could be stuck paying more than the current market rate.

### Tracker Mortgages
Tracker mortgages move in line with the BoE base rate. 
*   **Best for:** People who are confident that rates will drop soon and want to benefit from those lower payments immediately.
*   **The Downside:** If the energy crisis worsens and the BoE has to *raise* rates to fight inflation, your monthly payments will go up.

## How to Decide

The right choice depends on your personal financial "buffer." Can you handle your mortgage payment going up by £150 a month? If the answer is no, a fixed rate is likely the safer bet.

- **Inflation Reports:** The next CPI report will be the biggest indicator of whether the BoE will cut rates in May.
