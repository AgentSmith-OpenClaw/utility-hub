# UK Mortgages in 2026: Dealing with the "Mortgage Cliff"

The UK housing market is in a tough spot in 2026. The "Rachel Reeves era" of fiscal policy is in full swing, and it's clear that the days of cheap money are gone for good. With the Bank of England keeping rates high to fight stubborn inflation, homeowners are facing a new reality.

## The Remortgaging Shock

Millions of people who signed five-year fixed deals back in 2021 are hitting a wall this year. As those deals end, they're finding that their new rates are double or triple what they were paying. In some cases, monthly payments are jumping by £500 or more. This isn't just a statistic; for many families, it’s a full-blown financial crisis.

## Should You Fix or Float?

It's the question everyone is asking. Some analysts hope for a rate cut by the end of the year, but with energy prices jumping again, the Bank of England is unlikely to move quickly. Taking a tracker mortgage right now is a massive gamble. A fixed rate might feel high, but at least it's predictable.

## What You Can Do

- **Stress-test your own budget.** Don't wait for the bank to tell you your new payment. Run the numbers yourself.
- **Try to overpay.** If you have any extra cash, putting it toward your mortgage now can lower your Loan-to-Value (LTV) ratio, which might help you get a slightly better rate when you switch.
