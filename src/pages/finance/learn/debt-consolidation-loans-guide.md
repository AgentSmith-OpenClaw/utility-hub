# Best Practices for Debt Consolidation Loans in 2026 (US/UK/CA)

Credit card debt is suffocating. Interest rates are hovering around 24% globally, and paying the minimum balance is a mathematical trap designed to keep you broke. A debt consolidation loan can stop the bleeding, but only if you use it right.

## What is a Debt Consolidation Loan?

You take out a new personal loan with a lower interest rate to pay off your high-interest credit cards. Now you have one fixed monthly payment. It sounds simple, but banks love it when you fail. 

## The Trap

Here is what happens to most people: they consolidate $15,000 of credit card debt into a 5-year loan at 10%. They feel relieved. Their credit cards now have a zero balance. Three months later, they start using the cards again. Now they have a $300 monthly loan payment AND new credit card debt. 

Do not consolidate unless you cut up the cards or freeze them. Period.

## Getting the Best Rate

In the US and Canada, your credit score dictates everything. You need a FICO score above 680 to get a rate that makes consolidation worthwhile. In the UK, affordability checks are stricter this year, so expect lenders to scrutinize your bank statements.

Shop around. Local credit unions in the US and Canada usually offer better rates than massive national banks. In the UK, check the major comparison sites, but beware of soft checks vs hard searches.

## Alternatives

If your score is shot, a balance transfer card with a 0% introductory APR might be better, provided you can pay it off before the promotional period ends. Otherwise, check our [Budgeting Methods](/finance/learn/budgeting-methods-that-work) to free up cash.
