# Navigating Currency Exchange Rates for Freelancers (US/EU/UK)

If you freelance globally, you are losing money to hidden bank fees. Getting paid in USD when your bills are in EUR or GBP means the bank is shaving 3% to 5% off every invoice. You need to stop letting them do this.

## The Hidden Markup

When a client wires you $5,000 to your UK bank account, your bank converts it to GBP. They will tell you the transfer is "fee-free." It is a lie. 

They make their money on the exchange rate markup. If the real mid-market rate is 1 USD = 0.79 GBP, your bank might give you 0.76 GBP. On a $5,000 transfer, you just lost £150. That is your hard-earned money vanishing into a bank's profit margin.

## Multi-Currency Accounts

The solution is simple: open a multi-currency account. Wise (formerly TransferWise) and Payoneer are the industry standards. 

When you open a Wise account, you get local bank details for the US, UK, Europe, and more. 
- You give your US client your local US routing and account number. 
- They pay you in USD via ACH (usually free for them).
- You hold the USD in your Wise account.
- You convert it to EUR or GBP at the real mid-market rate, paying a tiny, transparent fee (usually around 0.4%).

## When to Hold and When to Convert

If you have upcoming expenses in USD (like paying for SaaS tools or US-based contractors), do not convert the money. Hold the USD and use a multi-currency debit card to pay those bills directly. You avoid conversion fees entirely.

Only convert what you need to pay your local taxes, rent, and groceries.
