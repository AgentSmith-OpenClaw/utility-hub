# Understanding Compound Interest: Daily vs. Monthly Compounding

Albert Einstein supposedly called compound interest the eighth wonder of the world. Whether he actually said it or not, the math doesn't lie. Compound interest is the engine of wealth creation. But not all compounding is created equal. The *frequency* of compounding—how often the interest is added to your principal—makes a massive difference over time.

## What is Compound Interest?

Simple interest pays you only on your initial deposit. Compound interest pays you on your initial deposit *and* on the interest you've already earned. It's interest on your interest.

You can model this easily using our [Compound Interest Calculator](/finance/calculators/compound-interest).

## Daily vs. Monthly vs. Annual

Let's look at how the compounding schedule changes your returns.

If you invest $10,000 at a 5% interest rate for 10 years:
- **Annually Compounded:** The interest is calculated once a year. After 10 years, you have $16,288.95.
- **Monthly Compounded:** The interest is calculated 12 times a year. After 10 years, you have $16,470.09.
- **Daily Compounded:** The interest is calculated 365 times a year. After 10 years, you have $16,486.65.

While the difference between monthly and daily compounding might seem small over 10 years, it scales dramatically with larger sums and longer time horizons. If you are saving for retirement over 30 or 40 years, daily compounding puts thousands of extra dollars in your pocket.

## Where to Find Favorable Compounding

- **High-Yield Savings Accounts (HYSAs):** Most top-tier online banks in the US and UK compound interest daily and pay it out monthly. Always check the terms before opening an account. 
- **Mortgages and Loans:** Compounding works against you when you borrow money. In Canada, fixed-rate mortgages are typically compounded semi-annually, which is actually favorable to the borrower compared to the US, where mortgages are often compounded monthly. You can compare these impacts using our [Mortgage Calculator](/finance/calculators/mortgage).
- **Credit Cards:** Credit cards are the dark side of compounding. They usually calculate interest daily based on your Average Daily Balance. This is why credit card debt spirals out of control so quickly. Try our [Credit Card Payoff Tool](/finance/calculators/credit-card-payoff) to see how quickly daily compounding can hurt you.

## Maximizing Your Returns

The formula for wealth is simple: invest early, invest often, and seek the highest compounding frequency possible for your savings.
