# Solar Tax Credits in the US: 2026 ROI Breakdown

Putting solar panels on your roof is not about saving the planet anymore. It's a math equation.

In 2026, the federal government is still handing out massive tax credits to make the math work for you, but the way utilities buy your extra power has changed drastically in states like California.

Here is the current state of solar ROI, how the 30% tax credit actually works, and why batteries are no longer optional.

## The 30% Federal Investment Tax Credit (ITC)

The Inflation Reduction Act locked in a 30% tax credit for solar installations through 2032. 

If you spend $20,000 putting panels on your house, the federal government gives you $6,000 back. 

### How the Credit Actually Works

This is a non-refundable tax credit, which confuses a lot of people. The IRS doesn't just cut you a check for $6,000. 

Instead, it reduces the federal income tax you owe for the year you turn the system on. 

*   **Scenario A:** You owe $8,000 in federal taxes this year. You apply the $6,000 credit. Now you only owe $2,000. If your employer already withheld $8,000 from your paychecks throughout the year, you get a $6,000 refund when you file.
*   **Scenario B:** You only owe $4,000 in federal taxes this year. You apply the credit, wiping out your $4,000 bill entirely. The remaining $2,000 of the credit rolls over to the next tax year.

You must have taxable income to use the ITC. If you are retired and living entirely on untaxed Social Security or Roth IRA distributions, this credit does nothing for you.

## Net Metering is Dying: Why You Need a Battery

For the last decade, solar math was simple: your panels make power during the day, you sell the extra to the grid at the retail rate, and you buy it back at night for the exact same price. The grid was your free battery.

That is over. 

States are moving to "Net Billing" (like NEM 3.0 in California). Utilities now pay you pennies for your extra daytime solar power, but still charge you full price—often peak pricing—to buy power back when the sun goes down.

### The Battery Equation

If you live in a state with gutted net metering, solar panels alone are a bad investment. You will send cheap power to the grid at 2 PM and buy expensive power at 7 PM. 

You need a home battery (like a Tesla Powerwall or Enphase IQ) to store your daytime power and use it at night. 

Yes, a battery adds $8,000 to $10,000 to your system cost. But the 30% ITC applies to the battery too. Even with the higher upfront cost, a solar + battery system in a high-electricity-cost state will usually pay for itself in 6 to 9 years. After that, your electricity is virtually free for the remaining 15+ years the system lasts.

## Should You Buy or Lease?

Never lease solar panels if you can avoid it. 

When you lease (or use a Power Purchase Agreement), the solar company gets the 30% tax credit, not you. They also put a lien on your house, which makes selling your home a nightmare because the buyer has to agree to take over your terrible lease.

If you don't have the cash, get a solar loan. You still own the system, you get the tax credit, and the monthly loan payment is often lower than your old utility bill.
