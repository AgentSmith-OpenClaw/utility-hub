# How to Calculate Compound Interest for Your Retirement (US & UK)

Everyone talks about compound interest like it's magic. It's not. It's just math. The earlier you start, the less you have to save. Let's break down exactly how it works for your US 401(k) or UK Pension, without the fluff.

## The Basic Formula

The formula is A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt). But you don't need a degree in finance to use it. 
- P is your starting balance.
- r is the annual interest rate (use 7% for a conservative stock market return after inflation).
- n is the number of times interest is compounded per year (usually 1 for annual stock returns).
- t is time in years.

If you put $10,000 in a low-cost index fund and leave it for 30 years at 7%, you get $76,122. You did nothing. The money just grew.

## Regular Contributions Change the Game

Most of us don't just drop a lump sum and walk away. We contribute monthly. If you add $500 a month to that initial $10,000, after 30 years, you have over $680,000. 

In the UK, auto-enrolment means your employer adds to your pension pot too. If you contribute 5% of your salary, your employer adds 3%. That's free money compounding over decades. Don't opt out.

## Action Steps

1. Check your employer match. In the US, if they match 4%, contribute at least 4%. In the UK, stick with the auto-enrolment minimums or higher.
2. Pick a broad market index fund. Forget stock picking.
3. Ignore the noise. The market drops, your balance drops. Leave it alone.
