# EU Travel Credit Cards: Navigating Zero FX Fees in 2026

Traveling across Europe means dealing with foreign transaction fees. Using your regular bank card abroad will drain 3% on every single purchase, plus whatever ATM fee they decide to stack on top.

Stop doing that. If you are crossing borders, you need a card that doesn't penalize you for spending your own money.

## The Problem with High Street Banks

Big traditional banks make billions off FX fees. They hide a 2.99% charge inside a slightly worse exchange rate. If you buy a €5 coffee in Paris with a standard UK debit card, you pay £4.30 instead of £4.10. That adds up fast on hotels, flights, and dinners.

You need a card that gives you the Mastercard or Visa wholesale exchange rate with absolute zero markup.

## Top Neobanks and Travel Cards

Here are the actual options that work right now without ridiculous annual fees.

### Monzo & Starling

These two still dominate the UK market for travel. Both offer zero foreign transaction fees. 

**Starling** is generally better because they don't limit your free overseas ATM withdrawals. **Monzo** gives you £200 fee-free every 30 days, then hits you with 3%. If you use cash in Germany or Spain, take the Starling card. If you just tap everywhere, either is fine.

### Revolut

Revolut is a giant in Europe. It's great for holding multiple currencies and converting them instantly on the app when the rate is good. 

But there's a catch: they charge a 1% markup on weekends because the currency markets are closed. If you use Revolut, exchange your money on Friday afternoon.

### Chase UK

Chase arrived a few years ago and is still aggressive. Zero FX fees, zero ATM fees abroad, and they currently give 1% cashback on your spending. It is probably the best all-rounder right now.

## Credit Cards vs Debit Cards

The cards above are mostly debit. If you want Section 75 protection (which legally protects your purchases over £100 if the airline goes bust or the hotel is a scam), you need a credit card.

*   **Barclaycard Rewards**: No annual fee, no foreign transaction fees, and it gives 0.25% cashback. It is boring but it works perfectly.
*   **Halifax Clarity**: The old reliable travel credit card. Still zero FX fees.

## Dynamic Currency Conversion (DCC)

This is the biggest scam in European travel. When you pay by card or use an ATM abroad, the machine will ask: "Do you want to pay in GBP or EUR?" 

**Always choose the local currency (EUR).** 

If you pick GBP, the foreign bank or ATM provider gets to invent their own exchange rate. It is always terrible. It will cost you 5-10% more than letting Visa or Mastercard do the conversion. 

Don't let them convert it for you. Tap the card, pick EUR, and let your zero-fee card handle the rest.
