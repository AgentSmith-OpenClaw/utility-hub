# The Last-Minute 2026 Tax Prep Guide: New Deductions and How to Claim Them

With the April 15th deadline approaching, US taxpayers have some new rules to deal with in 2026. Recent updates to tax laws have introduced a few specific deductions that are causing some confusion. If you're still getting your paperwork together, here is what you need to know to make sure you aren't overpaying.

## New Deductions for 2026

The most talked-about change is the Energy Credit for homeowners. If you upgraded to high-efficiency domestic energy systems in 2025, you can claim a significant credit this year. A helpful part of the 2026 rules is the "look-back" period. This means if you made a qualifying purchase in early 2025 but didn't claim it then, you might still be able to use it on this return.

There is also a new deduction for small business owners and freelancers who focus on domestic supply chains. If you bought at least 60% of your business hardware from US manufacturers, you likely qualify for the Small Business Continuity Deduction.

## E-Filing vs. Paper Returns

The IRS is currently reporting that paper returns are taking up to 14 weeks to process. E-filed returns with direct deposit are moving much faster, with most people getting their refunds in about 12 days. If you want your money back quickly, e-filing is the only real option.

## Getting the Math Right

Figuring out your adjusted gross income (AGI) is a bit harder this year because of these new variables. Standard spreadsheets usually don't account for the regional income limits that apply to the new credits.

## A Final Checklist
- **Check your 1099-DA:** The IRS is being very strict about digital asset reporting this year. If you traded crypto, make sure you have your forms ready.
- **State Tax Rules:** Not every state has adopted the new federal deductions. You'll need to check if your state allows these same credits.
- **Direct Deposit:** Double-check your bank info. A single wrong digit will delay your refund by weeks.

Tax season doesn't have to be a mess. If you stay on top of the new rules and use the right tools, you can get through it without the usual headache.
